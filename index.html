Miss Rumphius

Author: Barbara Cooney
Summary

The story follows Miss Rumphius a woman who travels the world, and fufills her dreams. She is told by her grandfather early in life that she can do anything she wants but she also has to do something to make the world more beautiful. Miss Rumphius keeps this in mind all through her life and finally comes up with an idea while she is in bed and unwell. She decides she will spread lupin seeds anywhere she can because they make everything more beautiful. She achieves this, and when she is much older she tells the next generation to again do something to make the world more beautiful.

Category: Picture Book
Appeal/Audience: American Book Award Winner
Limitations or weaknesses: Miss Rumphius is a beautiful written and illustrated book. There is some outdated language which some readers could find slightly offensive, but the message is still clear.
Assessment: "You must do something to make the world more beautiful" is a one of the main messages in Miss Rumphius and is great message to give to children. It is open-ended but very meaningful. Miss Rumphius or the Lupin Lady was a character I knew well as a children. While I do not remember reading the book, every spring my mom would tell us that the Lupin Lady had visited and sprinkled Lupin seeds all over our yard and the road we lived on. It was magical, and this book itself brings it own kind of magic as well.
Rating: ★★★★☆ 4/5 stars
APA Citation

Cooney, B. (1982). Miss Rumphius. Viking Press.

The Highway Man

Author: A: Alfred Noyes I: Charles Keeping
Summary

In 18th century England an unnamed highwayman comes to an inn. He is in love with the landlord of the inn's daughter, named Bess. The highwayman has to leave Bess and tells her he will be back soon to be with her. While he's gone a group of red-coat soldiers ambush Bess at the inn. Upon the highwayman's return to the inn, Bess sacrifices her life to warn him that the soldiers are there. The highwayman is killed shortly after by the soldiers. In the final part of the book, the highwayman and Bess meet again as ghosts.

Category: Picture Book
Secondary Category: Folktale, Poetry
Appeal/Audience: Older children
Limitations or weaknesses: This book is not suitable for younger children, there is a chilling nature to the writing and illustrations that could be off putting to younger children who may not grasp the story. It looks like children's picture book but it is much more than that.
Assessment: The Highwayman is beautifully written and illustrated. You don't often see horror or anything remotely like it in children's fiction it was interesting to read such a good example. I don't think this book would be for every child but some could really enjoy it especially between that tween to teen stage.
Rating: ★★★☆☆ 3/5 stars
APA Citation

Noyes, A. (1981). The highwayman. Oxford University Press.

Iktomi and the Berries

Author: Paul Goble
Summary

Iktomi, comes from Plain Lands and Lakota Creation stories. He is said to be a trickster, who uses his cleverness to sometimes help out rather than cause problems. In Iktomi and the Berries, Paul Goble retells a class Iktomi story where Iktomi sets out to hunt for food for his family and village so that they will be proud of him. Unfortunetly, he does not have good luck but eventually finds some berries that appear to be in the water. Iktomi jumps in the water grab them but he misses, and tries again with no luck. He decides to try anchoring himself to a rock to get them, instead the rock falls in the water with him and hits the bottom of the river. Iktomi sinks to the bottom as well, and must do whatever he can to escape.

Category: Fairytale/Folktale
Secondary Category: Picture Book
Appeal/Audience: Reading age 8 and under
Assessment: Iktomi and the Berries was a fun and entertaining read. Throughout the book, readers hear the narrated story as well as Iktomi's monologue and the narrators monologue. These elements add an interesting level of humour to the story.
Rating: ★★★★☆ 4/5 stars
APA Citation

Goble, P. (1989). Iktomi and the berries: A Plains Indian story. Orchard Books.

Dear Canada: No Safe Harbour

Author: Julie Lawson
Summary

No Safe Harbour: The Halifax Explosion Diary of Charlotte Blackburn is book fifteen in the Dear Canada series. This diary follows 12 year old Charlotte Blackburn and her experience surviving the Halifax Explosion. If I Ran The Zoo

If I Ran The Zoo follows Gerald McGrew, he visits a zoo and begins thinking about what he would include in his zoo if he was in charge. I had never read it before, and I surprised to see it included on so many banned or challenged book lists but it is definitely cause for concern. The caricatures and overall content throughout the book could be harmful to readers.

Seuss, Dr. (1950). If I ran the zoo. Random House.

Fatty Legs

The story begins when Margaret is eight years old when a residential school opens up near her home. Margaret begs her parents to go to the school because she dreams of learning how to read. The main source of her bullying is coming from a group of girls who used to be friends, but they no longer see her that way. We get immersed in Hélène's fiction world that she uses to protect herself from the bullying she is experiencing. She compares her life to Jane Erye and what she faced. Eventually, during a class trip, Hélène meets a real friend named Géraldine, she shows Hélène that she is worthy of love and friendship and things begin to look up for her.

Category: Graphic
Appeal/Audience: Reading age 8-12
Assessment: Jane, the Fox and Me deals with a lot of topics and issues that I faced as a child. It is emotional, beautiful, and shows what happens when you get stuck in your own head.
